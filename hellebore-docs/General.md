 Stardew occult survival

In a loop improve your base and then survive the night as spirits and whatnot penetrate it. Trade-off between defence and income. Some stuff might be dual use like crops you can hide in. Imagine seeing your field, possibly the one you're hiding in, engulfed in spectral fire at 2am. Natural terrain prevents you making it completely regular and boring. You can't just hide in one spot. Maybe because you want to kill things, maybe because you need to protect your base but probably just because it's dangerous to stay in one spot and get cornered as ghosts surround you and then come through your walls. 

This is a fairly natural thing. It should be possible to build naturally and watch the good stuff emerge. It's an interesting scenario so just creating a simulator for it is motivating in itself.

Naturally, you will want to take care of threats as soon as possible, before they can gather. 

Perhaps orchards for example are lucrative but very dangerous at night. You might not go into them voluntarily but you might go in to escape something else, like Merry and Pippin.

What we most want to do is avoid making it two separate games, which would happen if the base design had no impact on or trade-off with survivability. Budget and space means there will always be some trade-off. You could also have adjacency effects and enemies that do very well in certain terrains.

If there needs to be an incentive for the player to venture out into danger consider having enemies drop loot (so long as they don't get farmed or say goodbye to the vibe) or tasks to do or items that spawn at night like stars in Terraria. But none of this may be necessary if the enemy AI keeps the player on the move.

By bringing in new enemies while adding features like healing waters or lamps that can detect certain enemies, each round is different to the last. Passives are also possible, either instead or as well as.

Unlike tower defence the mission is not to protect the base, but to survive. If it was about the base you'd be constantly running everywhere which is annoying.

It's more like the hideout in Darkwood but here the area is larger, you're expected to be mobile and you're in something you built yourself.

The trade-off with other survival or horror games:
- **Survival**: You lose exploration but in return you get stakes. The player is defending something they value. Plus, with an emphasis on terrain rather than crafting better gear, enemies can be much more interesting.
- **Horror**: You get a way to do progression without ruining the sense of threat by making the player powerful. It's done to the environment instead.
- **Tower defence**: Much more engaging when the enemies come, defence design is more interesting and is something you engage with personally, you don't have to watch helplessly if you realise your setup wasn't quite enough.
